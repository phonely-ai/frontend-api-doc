Call routing in Phonely controls where a conversation goes next, whether that means escalating to a human, moving the caller to a different conversational path, or gracefully handling situations where no one is available.

Unlike traditional IVR systems that rely on rigid menus and deep branching trees, Phonely uses intent-aware routing. The agent continuously understands what the caller wants and dynamically routes the call without breaking the conversation.

This makes routing feel natural to callers while keeping workflows clean and easy to maintain.

## Why Routing Matters (Even With AI)

While Phonely can automate the majority of conversations, real-world use cases often require human intervention or specialized handling, such as:

- A customer asking about something the AI hasn’t been trained on
- A frustrated caller requesting a human
- A sales lead needing a live handoff
- A support request that belongs to a specific team
- Calls that should behave differently based on time of day

Routing ensures the right outcome happens at the right moment, without forcing callers through menus or restarting calls.

<Info>
  Escalation is not a fallback of last resort. It is a deliberate part of your call design, ensuring every customer journey has a safe path to resolution.
</Info>

## How Routing Works

Routing in Phonely is built around **workflows (flows)**. A call always starts in one flow, but it doesn’t have to stay there. Depending on what the caller says, what the AI detects, or what conditions you define, Phonely can:

- Listens continuously to the conversation
- Detects intent, sentiment, and keywords
- Decides when a routing action is needed
- Executes the routing seamlessly
- Fall back gracefully if no one is available

This makes routing both **predictable** and **adaptive**, without locking you into rigid IVR-style menus

From the caller’s perspective, it feels like a single uninterrupted conversation, even when the system is switching paths or escalating behind the scenes.

## Routing Outcomes in Phonely

At a high level, routing in Phonely leads to one of two outcomes:

1. **The call is routed to a human or external destination**
2. **The call is routed to another internal workflow**

Both happen without ending the call, and both are handled using purpose-built routing blocks that centralize logic instead of spreading it across large flows.

## Transfer Calls

When a call needs to be handed off to a person or department, Phonely performs a live call transfer.

This routing approach is commonly used for:

- Customer support escalation
- Sales handoffs
- Requests for specific people
- Situations where the AI determines human help is required

Phonely does more than simply forward the call. It can:

- Decide _who_ should receive the call based on what the caller says
- Try multiple destinations automatically if someone doesn’t answer
- Control whether the handoff is immediate or introduced with context
- Manage what the caller hears while waiting
- Detect whether a person, voicemail, or IVR answered
- Gracefully recover if no one is available

All of this happens within a single routing action, instead of requiring complex branching logic.

Phonely supports two kinds of transfers, both available through the [Transfer Call](/key-concepts/blocks/live-call-actions/transfer-flow-new) block.

## Transfer Flows

Not all routing leads to a human. In many scenarios, the correct outcome is to move the caller to a different conversational flow, such as:

- Switching from appointment scheduling to billing
- Moving from general inquiries to technical support
- Escalating from self-service to a structured escalation flow

In these cases, Phonely routes the caller **between workflows**, while keeping the call active and the conversation coherent.

This allows teams to design modular, reusable flows that can be shared across agents and updated in one place, without duplicating logic.

## Global Transfer Flows

Transfers don’t always happen in predictable places. A caller may ask for support halfway through an appointment booking flow, or the AI may detect frustration at any point. For these cases, Phonely supports Global Flows.

A **global flow** can be triggered at any point during a conversation, regardless of which flow the caller is currently in. When a global condition is met, Phonely immediately exits the current flow and routes into the global one.

This is ideal for escalation scenarios, such as:

- The caller asks to speak to a human
- The caller sounds frustrated
- The caller asks for support, sales, or a specific person

For example, if a caller says:

> “I want to talk to support”\
> or\
> “This is frustrating, can I speak to someone?”

Phonely can instantly route them into a **Transfer Flow**, even if they’re mid-conversation in another workflow. [Learn how you can configure global routing here](blocks/live-call-actions/transfer-flow-new).

## Intelligent Decision-Making During Routing

A key difference between Phonely and traditional systems is **how routing decisions are made**.

Routing decisions are not based on hard-coded branches. Instead, they are driven by:

- Natural language conditions (described in plain text)
- AI interpretation of caller intent
- Clarifying questions when intent is ambiguous
- Global routing rules applied consistently across workflows

This allows routing logic to evolve naturally as your business grows, without requiring constant rewiring of flows.

## Handling Failure, Wait Time, and Edge Cases

Routing isn’t just about success paths, it’s also about handling what happens when things don’t go as planned.

Phonely routing supports:

- Automatic retries when a destination doesn’t answer
- Intelligent fallbacks when no one is available
- Custom messaging during wait time
- Detection of voicemail vs real humans
- Safe exits back into the conversation if a transfer fails

The caller is never dropped into silence or dead ends. If a route fails, the agent continues the conversation naturally and guides the caller to the next best outcome.

## Routing Based on Time and Availability

Sometimes routing should only happen during specific times.

For example:

- Transfer calls only between 9:00 AM and 9:00 PM
- Handle after-hours calls differently

To do this, use a **Time Filter** block.

The Time Filter lets you define:

- Days of the week
- Time windows
- Timezone-aware behavior

Inside business hours, you can transfer the call. Outside business hours, you can:

- Inform the caller no one is available
- Take a message
- Send an email or Slack notification
- Schedule a callback

This keeps routing realistic and avoids sending callers to unanswered lines.

## Handling Transfer Failures Gracefully

Not every transfer succeeds, and Phonely lets you plan for that.

Within the **Transfer Call** block, you can define what happens if:

- No one answers
- Voicemail is detected
- The transfer fails
- An IVR system is reached

In these cases, you can route the call to:

- A Talk block to explain the situation
- A Collect block to take a message
- An Email or Slack notification
- A callback or follow-up workflow

This ensures the caller is never left hanging.

## Advanced Options

Transfer Call blocks come with additional controls that improve reliability.

| **Option**              | **Type**  | **Description**                                                                                                       |
| :---------------------- | :-------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Voicemail Detection** | `boolean` | Detects voicemail and brings the caller back into the flow with a fallback message                                    |
| **Transfer Failures**   | `action`  | Defines what happens if a transfer cannot connect - for example, retry once or send the call to a fallback Talk block |
| **IVR Detection**       | `boolean` | Detects if the destination number has an IVR (phone tree) and handles it appropriately                                |

These options ensure that escalation is never the end of the conversation. Even when transfers fail, callers remain supported.

## Best Practices

Phonely’s transfer features are flexible, but good design makes them effective. A few guidelines:

- Use warm transfers in customer-facing scenarios where context matters.
- Always configure fallbacks so that no call ends in silence.
- Apply Time Filters or other conditions to avoid unnecessary transfers.
- Treat escalation as part of the customer journey, not an exception to it.

<Warning>
  Failing to set fallbacks can result in dropped calls. Always define what should happen if a transfer does not complete.
</Warning>

Call routing, transfers, and escalation transform AI automation from a closed system into a flexible, human-aware experience. Cold transfers are fast, warm transfers are professional, Global Flows provide responsiveness at any point, and Time Filters give you control over when transfers should occur. By layering in advanced options such as voicemail detection and transfer failure handling, you can design flows that never leave a caller unsupported.
